## What it does

`tech-walkthrough` takes a plan whose business decisions are already made and shows **what is going to happen in the code**, on a single Excalidraw board: the before and after of each flow, the map of the systems that talk to each other, the backstage (queue, worker, retries, where the result lands), the migration with its why, the performance verdict, and the risks that remain. The chat gets the link and three lines; everything else is drawn.

It does not explain the implementation you would have written first. Before anything is drawn, three agents with different biases (minimal, robust, performance) propose, critique and converge on the implementation, and the board explains the one that came out of the debate, with the alternatives it discarded and the failure points each agent found.

Every piece of code is introduced by what it does, with the technical name after it ("the package that keeps the local copy of orders, `packages/mirror`"). The skill assumes you do not hold the whole codebase in your head.

## When to reach for it

Type `/tech-walkthrough`, or the agent reaches for it on its own when you ask to understand the technical side, ask whether there is a migration, or ask whether something will get slow.

Use it **after** planning (`/grill-me`, `/grill-with-docs`, a spec, an issue) and **before** implementing. If the business plan is still open, it is too early: go back to grilling. If what you want is the task list rather than the understanding, use [to-tickets](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills/blob/main/skills/engineering/to-tickets/SKILL.md).

| Situation | What to do |
|---|---|
| Delivery with an open technical decision (new queue or reused one, table or free-form field, library or own code) | `/tech-walkthrough`: the council decides |
| Small delivery, nothing open (one route, one field) | `/tech-walkthrough fast`: no debate, you decide, risks in a single pass |
| Delivery too big for one board (more than three flows changing) | Split by flow and run once per flow |

## Prerequisites

- Python 3 on your PATH: the board is generated by `scripts/excalidraw_build.py` from a spec.
- An open repository: the spec and the `.excalidraw` live in `docs/walkthroughs/` so the board can be adjusted later.
- Claude Code's Artifact tool, to publish the board as a link. Without it, the skill delivers only the `.excalidraw` file, which opens in excalidraw.com, VS Code or Obsidian.

## The technical council

The implementation is chosen through a debate in rounds: each agent proposes from its own bias after reading the code, receives the other two proposals and answers what it accepts and what it keeps (an objection only counts with a fact from the code or a concrete scenario), and finally lists what can still break in the settled implementation. The orchestrator consolidates: facts beat opinions, ties of taste go to the minimal option, and what the plan already decided is never reopened silently.

The debate is not on the board. What is on the board is its result: the **How we got to this implementation** section (decision, choice, discarded, why) and the **Risks** section (verdict, where the council disagrees with the plan, failure points by severity, what held up).

## The backstage

The "queue" box in a flow hides exactly the part you cannot picture on your own: who publishes and how many messages, through which queue and with what guarantee (ordering, retries), who consumes, where the result lands, what happens when the retries run out. The skill opens that box in its own section whenever an earlier diagram mentions a queue, worker, job, webhook or retry, without waiting for you to ask.

## Common questions

**Why Excalidraw and not text?**
Because the problem the skill solves is one of reading, not of information: the technical content was already in the plan's paragraphs, and it still was not possible to see what changed. Boxes, arrows and colours with a fixed meaning (grey already exists, red is the problem, green is new, dashed happens later) carry that in seconds.

**Can I edit the board?**
Yes. The published page is the real Excalidraw, and the `.excalidraw` opens in any compatible editor. To change the content and keep the link, edit the spec under `docs/walkthroughs/` and run the skill again; it republishes to the same artifact.

**The council takes a while. Is it always worth it?**
No. It exists for open technical decisions. For a small delivery, `fast` skips the debate and costs a single pass.

**Is the risks section a separate self-review?**
No. It is the last round of the same debate, consolidated: high-severity points are checked in the code by the orchestrator before they go on the board.

## It's working if

- You understand what changes by looking at the before and after, without reading the table.
- Every box carries the real name you will find in the code or the console (queue, worker, route), not an invented one.
- The backstage section showed up without you asking, whenever a queue or job was on the path.
- The migration has a why you could repeat to someone else.
- The chat is four lines long, and the rest is on the board.

## Where it fits

A chain step: `grill-me` or `grill-with-docs` → **tech-walkthrough** → `to-tickets` or `implement`. It comes in when the business side is decided and goes out with the implementation chosen and drawn; the items where the council disagrees with the plan go back into the plan before becoming tickets.
